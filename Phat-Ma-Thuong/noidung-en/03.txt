CHUONG: Late, forgetful, no homework
EPI: Forgetting and lateness are the easiest faults to repeat. So a good punishment helps the student remember, not fear.
LEAD: Arriving late, forgetting books, forgetting homework, forgetting cleaning duty: faults that come every week. Punish heavily and you tire yourself; punish lightly and the fault returns. What you need is a way for the student to feel the real cost of forgetting, along with tools to remember. This chapter focuses on punishments that build a habit within them.

## A student is late several times in one week
LOI: Phuc has been late four times this week. Each time he apologises in a small voice and says traffic. You have reminded him many times and still he is late. You do not yet know the real cause.
CHIEU: Entry ticket | Each time he is late, he receives a small ticket and fills in two lines himself: the real reason, and one thing he will do differently tomorrow. He puts the ticket in the box on your desk. Three tickets in a week and you invite him to sit for five minutes at break to look together at what the three have in common. Anyone who arrives five minutes early five days in a row gets his tickets returned and cleared, with a public word of praise.
THAY: Phuc, I will not punish you with scolding. Each time you are late, write these two lines on a ticket: the real reason, and what you will do differently tomorrow. Just be honest.
TRO: Um... my home is far, and Mum leaves early for work, so I have to make my own breakfast.
THAY: Thank you for telling me the truth. Let us find a way to get you here on time, starting with breakfast.
VS: Lateness has causes, and often they lie beyond the student's control. The ticket makes him think about the cause instead of just apologising. With three tickets, you see the pattern and can really help.
RG: Do not punish when the cause is family or transport he cannot decide. Do not read tickets aloud. Do not use tickets as evidence for discipline.
BT: Add a reward column: five minutes early three days running earns the pick of seats that week.
CN: I need the real reason in order to help you. Write it on the ticket and we will find a way together.

## A student forgets the textbook
LOI: Ha has forgotten her book for the third time. She shares with her neighbour; it does not disrupt much, but the neighbour is inconvenienced, and you want her to take responsibility.
CHIEU: The teacher's lending library | Lend her your own book, for a small fee: she serves as reading assistant and reads the opening passage of today's lesson aloud. If she is shy, let her read silently and summarise in one sentence. At the end of the lesson she returns the book and names one thing she remembers. If she forgets again, she chooses a small way to make amends, such as labelling books for the class library.
THAY: Ha forgot her book, so I will lend you mine. The borrowing fee is that you read the opening passage aloud to the class. Read it clearly for me.
TRO: Um... I will read it.
THAY: Thank you. That was very clear. Next time, shall we borrow again, or will you put the book in your bag the night before?
VS: Forgetting a book is usually an unprepared evening. The fee is a useful task, not a shame, and tied to the very lesson. She also practises reading aloud, so the price is light and beneficial at once.
RG: Do not mock her publicly or press her on why she forgot. If she has no book because her family struggles, do not punish; give real support with books.
BT: Let her write her own list of three things to remember the book and stick it in her bag.
CN: I will lend you my book. Will you read the opening passage aloud for the class?

## A student did not do the homework
LOI: Tien did not do his homework twice this week. He says: I forgot. You do not know whether he truly forgot or thinks it unimportant.
CHIEU: The promise slip | Have him sign a small promise slip: the date and time he will hand it in. Say clearly: hand it in on time and there is no deduction, and I will add a point for keeping your word. If late, he gives the reason and hands in an extra question of his own about that topic. The slip stays in your book until the end of the month so he sees you trust him.
THAY: Tien, I do not want to deduct marks. Sign this promise slip: you promise to hand it in tomorrow afternoon. On time and I will praise you as well. Does that work?
TRO: Yes, sir. I will hand it in on time.
THAY: I trust you. And if something is hard, see me before the deadline.
VS: Students who forget homework often fear the consequence more than the task. The slip lets him choose the date, so he sees himself as responsible, and gives you a signed agreement. When he keeps his word, he judges himself differently.
RG: Do not use the slip for a student in serious trouble at home. Do not show it before the class. If he forgets constantly for weeks, ask directly whether he needs help.
BT: Let him choose a classmate as witness of the slip; the friend only witnesses, and does not remind in his place.
CN: Sign the promise slip. I trust you to hand it in on the day.

## A student forgets cleaning duty
LOI: A whole group forgot cleaning duty, and Khoi has forgotten two weeks running. The room still has litter and the board is unwiped. Others are beginning to complain.
CHIEU: A day as class steward | Invite Khoi to be class steward the next day. He comes ten minutes early, does a round to check the board, desks, windows, lights, fans, and reports back in ten words. He wears a special pen on his pocket marked Steward. All day he sees how complicated cleaning duty is. Afterwards, he draws up his own reminder list for his group.
THAY: Khoi, tomorrow you are class steward for me. Come ten minutes early, do a round of checks, then report to me. There is a Steward pen, wear it proudly.
TRO: Um... I can do it, sir.
THAY: I trust you. Whatever the class needs, report it to me.
VS: Cleaning is the job nobody wants to remember. When he becomes the one responsible for everything, he understands the value of each small task. The steward title shows it is a role with standing, not a punishment.
RG: Do not use cleaning as a fixed punishment, or students will come to hate tidying. Do not make him work alone for more than twenty minutes.
BT: A group that turns up in full for cleaning three days running chooses a piece of music to play at break.
CN: Tomorrow you are class steward for me; please come ten minutes early.

## A student forgets equipment for a practical lesson
LOI: The practical lesson needs a ruler and compasses and Linh has none. She sits watching the others work and does not dare say so. You notice, but she keeps her head down, clearly embarrassed.
CHIEU: String around the finger | Do not scold; hand her a small sticky note and say: stick this on your desk at home so you remember tomorrow. Ask what she wants written on it. She writes it herself and you sign beneath it as witness. Then lend her the equipment you have ready. Next week ask whether the note is still stuck up.
THAY: Linh, borrow my ruler first. Then I give you this note: write what you need to bring and stick it on your desk at home.
TRO: Um... thank you, sir.
THAY: Next week I will ask whether the note is still there.
VS: Forgetting is a failure of memory, not attitude. A memory tool solves the actual root, many times more effective than reproach. Your signature as witness makes her feel trusted.
RG: Do not punish forgetting equipment when the family cannot afford to buy it. Do not tell the class she forgot.
BT: Give the whole class one minute at the start of each lesson to check bags, with anyone missing something writing it on a note by the desk.
CN: Forgetting happens, and we have a way to remember. Stick this on your desk at home.
