CHUONG: Vandalism, scribbling, damaging property
EPI: A defaced desk is a message from the student. Read the message first, wipe the desk afterwards.
LEAD: Drawing on desks, carving chairs, breaking fans, throwing paper around: faults you see at once because they leave a mark. Punishing with money or lines rarely teaches anything. What works is letting the student repair what he spoiled, in a meaningful way that ends with some pride. This chapter offers moves of that kind.

## A student draws on the desk
LOI: You find a desktop covered in doodles and a few lines of writing. After a few days you learn it was Lam. He does not own up at once, but bows his head when you ask gently.
CHIEU: The artist pays back the desk | Praise first: your drawing has real spirit. Then assign the task: clean the desktop with cleaner at break, with you sitting beside him. When done, invite him to paint a small picture or a short poem on the class noticeboard, free to draw as he likes. He mends the fault and also has a place to express himself, so he no longer needs the desk.
THAY: Lam, your drawing really has spirit. But the desk belongs to the whole class. Clean it at break, I will sit with you. Afterwards I give you the class noticeboard to draw on, as freely as you like.
TRO: Um... I will clean it right away. I am sorry.
THAY: Thank you. I look forward to seeing your picture on the board.
VS: He draws on the desk because he needs somewhere to express himself. Making him clean it pays for the act; giving him a legitimate place to draw meets the root need. Praise first helps him listen without defending.
RG: Do not treat obscene or insulting content as a joke. If the drawing contains a threat or insults a specific person, deal with it more seriously and protect the person targeted.
BT: Open a creative wall in the class, with one student in charge of decoration each week.
CN: Your drawing has spirit. Clean the desk and let us find you a place to draw freely.

## A student damages class equipment
LOI: The ceiling fan makes a strange noise; it turns out someone curious tampered with the switch and broke it. The board is scratched by a group fooling around. Nobody owns up, but a few seem to know.
CHIEU: A maintenance slip | Tell the class: broken things are not for finding someone to blame but for finding a way to mend. Write a small maintenance slip on the board, noting the broken item and how to fix it. Anyone who knows something writes a name and offers to help repair; you do not yet ask who broke it. At the end of the week they work with the caretaker on small repairs and learn to turn a screw. If anyone owns up, you value honesty above the act.
THAY: Broken things we mend; I have not asked who did it. Whoever wants to help repair, write your name on this slip, and at the end of the week we learn to turn screws with the caretaker.
TRO: Um... I would like to help, sir. Actually I did fiddle with the switch.
THAY: Thank you for owning up. I value your honesty, and you help the caretaker.
VS: Pressing for the culprit frightens the student into hiding. Shifting the focus to repair makes the one responsible speak up, because he is honoured as a helper. Learning repair also shows students the value of objects.
RG: Do not impose money compensation on students if rules do not allow it. Do not let students do dangerous work like high ladders or electricity. If the damage is large, inform parents as regulations require.
BT: At the end of the term, award the class a badge of pride for taking care of its equipment.
CN: Broken things we mend together. Whoever wants to help the caretaker, write your name.

## The class throws paper balls and paper planes
LOI: Break has just ended and the classroom floor is strewn with crumpled paper and paper planes. One student has hit a classmate on the head. The class is laughing loudly. You walk in on the mess.
CHIEU: The paper ball court | Smile, put a cardboard box in the middle of the room and say: if the whole class throws that well, let us make it a contest. Each student throws one sheet into the box from their seat. Each that lands scores a point for the class. Then everyone gathers the rest of the paper, and you invite them to race to fold paper planes from clean paper and hand them in for decorations. The class moves from wrecking to playing by rules and then tidies up.
THAY: If our class throws that well, let us make it a contest! Each throws one sheet into this box from your seat, and each that goes in scores a point for the class. Afterwards the whole class gathers the paper.
TRO: (Laughing.) You really let us compete, sir?
THAY: Really. But when we finish, we tidy up in two minutes.
VS: Paper-throwing is energy with nowhere to go. A contest with rules turns wrecking into play, so students sense the stopping point themselves. Ending with tidying shows them their responsibility.
RG: Do not allow sharp or heavy objects to be thrown. Do not let the contest drag into noisy chaos. If a student is hurt by a throw, stop and care for that student first.
BT: Use the gathered paper to make recycled products, like a class pencil holder.
CN: If we throw this well, let us compete with rules and then tidy up together.

## A student scrawls on the toilet wall
LOI: The caretaker reports an insulting line about a classmate written on the toilet wall. The class whispers. The student mentioned is very upset but does not want to speak.
CHIEU: Wipe the wall, write the words | This move is more serious and done in private. You and the school erase the writing at once to protect the student. You do not investigate before the class but put a small slip on each desk: whoever has information is asked to tell you privately, and whoever wrote it may come to see you. A student who owns up, besides cleaning the wall, writes an unsigned letter of apology to the student named; you pass it on and keep it confidential.
THAY: (To the whole class, gently.) I have had the writing on the wall erased. Whoever knows something or wrote it, please come to me privately. I will keep it confidential and we will put it right together.
TRO: (One student comes privately.) Sir, it was me who wrote it... I am sorry.
THAY: Thank you for coming. Write an unsigned letter of apology to that student and I will pass it on. And whoever else heard about it, I need your help too, to support that friend.
VS: Protecting the person targeted comes first, so wipe it at once. Keeping confidentiality for the one who owns up lets him step forward without fearing the class's laughter. The unsigned letter lets him make amends without public humiliation.
RG: If the content is serious, such as threats, harassment or targeting gender, report to the school and handle under regulations. Do not make the targeted student meet the writer. Do not guess the culprit.
BT: Add a session on words that hurt friends, naming nobody.
CN: I have had the writing erased. Whoever knows or wrote it, come see me privately and I will keep it confidential.

## A student carves his name into a tree in the school yard
LOI: At break, a group of students carve their names with ballpoint pens into the trunk of the flame tree and boast to each other. The tree has a long scar. The caretaker is very sad, and so are you.
CHIEU: Guardians of the tree | Ask the group: do you know how old this tree is? Assign the task: for a month the group becomes the tree's guardians. Each week water it twice, pick up fallen leaves around its base, and photograph how it changes. At the end of the month write a short bulletin about the tree. A small sign by the trunk reads: this tree is cared for by the group. The names remain, but now they are the names of those who care.
THAY: You carved your names on the tree, so from now on you are the tree's guardians for a month. Water it, gather the leaves, photograph the changes. At the end of the month write a bulletin about the tree for the class.
TRO: Um... we can do that, sir.
THAY: I trust you. Your names will be on a small sign at the foot of the tree, as the ones who look after it.
VS: He carves his name to leave a mark. You keep the need to leave a mark and turn it positive. Caring creates attachment, so the students no longer want to hurt the tree.
RG: Do not make them work in the burning sun. Do not use the tree as a means to humiliate them. If the tree is badly damaged, report to the school to handle it properly.
BT: The whole class names the tree together and draws a map of the school's trees.
CN: You carved your names on the tree, so from now on you are its guardians.
