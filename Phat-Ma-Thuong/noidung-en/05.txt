CHUONG: Phones, games and screens in class
EPI: Ban phones and students hide them. Understand them and students put them away themselves.
LEAD: The phone is a contest teachers cannot win by absolute prohibition, because it sits in the students' pockets and in our own lives. This chapter offers ways to keep the phone in its place during lessons while keeping the relationship, with clear limits for heavier matters such as secret filming.
## A student uses a phone under the desk
LOI: Quan bends over his desk drawer for most of the lesson, thumb scrolling constantly. You have reminded him twice; he puts it away, and a few minutes later takes it out again. Those around him have seen it all.
CHIEU: The phone goes to rest | Bring a box lined with a cloth, called the phone hotel. Say: your phone looks tired, let it rest in the hotel until the end of the lesson. He puts the phone in the box himself and you give him a room card. At the end of the lesson he swaps the card for his phone. On the third time in a week, the hotel keeps the phone until the end of the day and he himself messages his mother that he needs to collect it.
THAY: Quan, your phone looks as if it needs a rest. Put it in my hotel until the end of the lesson, I give you a room card. At the end you swap it back.
TRO: Um... I will put it in.
THAY: Thank you. It will feel refreshed after resting.
VS: He places the phone in the box himself, so it does not feel like confiscation. A touch of humour lowers the tension, and the room card shows it is a fair, clear process. By messaging his mother on the third time he takes responsibility himself.
RG: Do not look at the phone's content. Do not keep it overnight. Do not let the phone fall or break; keep it in a lined box.
BT: Give the whole class a hotel day at the start of the week; anyone who deposits voluntarily receives a stamp.
CN: Your phone needs a short rest. Let us put it in my hotel.

## A group at the back plays games
LOI: Four students at the back sit close, heads tilted and hands covering, clearly playing a game together. They glance toward the board and then bend down again. You do not want to name the whole group before the class.
CHIEU: A game quiz in words | At break, invite the group over. Say: I do not know how to play, so teach me. Each student teaches you one skill from the game in two minutes. Then ask the group to prepare a two-minute talk for the class at the end of the week on something that can be learned from games: teamwork, strategy, managing resources. Caught up in the preparation, they no longer play during lessons.
THAY: You play together really well. I want to learn. At break teach me for two minutes each, and by the weekend give the class a talk on one thing games teach you.
TRO: You will really let us give a talk about games, sir?
THAY: Really. But it must contain something learned, and last two minutes. I am waiting.
VS: Ban games and students play secretly. Making games a topic of study shows them their interest has value and at the same time makes them see they must manage their playing in lessons. You also step into their world.
RG: Do not choose a heavily violent game as the topic. Do not let the talk turn into an advertisement. Do it once per group only, not as a reward for playing.
BT: Have the group act as coaches teaching a classmate to play within five minutes of break.
CN: I want to learn games from you. Teach me for two minutes at break.

## A student secretly films or photographs the teacher or classmates
LOI: You notice Tuan holding up his phone, filming you teaching, or secretly photographing a classmate dozing off. He laughs and says it is just a joke. But the clip could be posted online.
CHIEU: Delete it together | Ask him to hand over the phone, and delete the clip together right in front of you, checking the trash too. Say plainly, with no joking: I understand you only thought it was fun, but a secretly filmed clip can hurt the person in it and is hard to pull back once posted. He writes a short page on how the filmed person might feel. If the clip was of a classmate, he apologises to that classmate in person, without pressing the classmate to forgive.
THAY: Tuan, I understand you meant it as a joke. But that clip could hurt the person in it, and it is very hard to take back. Let us delete it together right now, show me the trash as well.
TRO: Um... I am sorry, sir.
THAY: Write me one short page about how the person in the clip might feel, and if it is a classmate, tell them privately.
VS: Secret filming is much more serious than he imagines. Deleting at once removes the risk, and the page helps him put himself in the other's place. You keep a serious tone without shaming him, so he can listen.
RG: If the clip has already been posted or has spread, report to the school and family at once, do not handle it alone. Do not publicise his name. Do not press the filmed classmate to forgive.
BT: Invite him and the whole class to draft together a short set of rules on filming in class, written by the students themselves.
CN: We delete it together now. Then write me a page on how the person in the clip might feel.

## A student checks the phone every ten minutes
LOI: Vy does not play games or text much, but every ten minutes she picks up her phone, glances, and puts it away. She says she does not know why she does it. The whole lesson is broken up.
CHIEU: The touch counter | Give Vy a small card with squares. Each time she touches the phone during a lesson, she marks a square herself; nobody checks. At day's end she counts and writes the number. Three days later ask: which number was highest, and what do you think pulls your hand? Together set a goal of cutting down gradually, say two fewer a day.
THAY: Vy, I am not asking you to give up your phone. Try marking this card each time you touch it for three days, nobody will check. Then we see what the numbers say.
TRO: Um... I can just count, sir?
THAY: You can. Count honestly; I only want to know whether you find that number high or low.
VS: Reaching for the phone is a reflex, not an intention. Counting turns the reflex into awareness, so counting alone lowers the number. She sets her own goal, so she does not feel forced.
RG: Do not demand to see the card if she does not want to. Do not label her addicted. If the number is very high and affects study and mood, talk to the family about seeing a counsellor.
BT: Do it with her for a week: you also count your phone touches and share.
CN: Try counting how many times you touch your phone over three days, then we will look at the number together.

## A student uses a phone to look up answers during a test
LOI: In the middle of a fifteen-minute test, you see Lam sneak his phone out under the desk. He bends very low. When you approach, he puts it away, his face bright red.
CHIEU: Two papers and one honest word | Say nothing publicly. At the end of the lesson, keep him back privately. Say: I am holding your paper. Tomorrow you redo an equivalent paper in ten minutes in front of me, and both marks are recorded. Ask one question: what made you want to look up the answers, fear of a low mark, or not having revised in time? If he is honest, help him plan revision.
THAY: Lam, I saw you use your phone. I will not tell the class. Tomorrow redo an equivalent paper in front of me. Right now I want to ask: were you afraid of a low mark, or had you not had time to revise?
TRO: Um... I was afraid my parents would shout at me, sir.
THAY: Thank you for telling the truth. Let us find a way to revise with less pressure.
VS: Cheating in a test is often fear more than intent. Letting him redo it in front of you and asking the real cause shows you help rather than punish. Keeping it quiet leaves his dignity intact and his wish to mend.
RG: Do not announce it. If school rules demand a formal procedure, follow them, but still talk to him privately. Do not use marks as a weapon.
BT: Give him a chance to mark his own earlier paper and note where he is weak.
CN: I am holding your paper and telling no one. Redo it tomorrow, and I want to know what you were worried about.
