CHUONG: Talking back, sulking, stubbornness
EPI: A student who dares to argue still trusts that you will listen. The silent student is the one to worry about.
LEAD: Being argued with before the class is when teachers lose their calm most easily, because it feels as if authority is being challenged. But it is also when a skilful response turns a student from opponent to partner. This chapter offers moves that protect your standing while keeping the door open for the student to speak, with the limits to hold when things go too far.

## A student argues back in front of the class
LOI: You tell Phat to sit up properly. He retorts: I did nothing, sir, why do you always pick on me? His voice is raised and the whole class falls silent, watching teacher and student.
CHIEU: Postponing the argument | Take a breath and say calmly: I hear you and I want to hear it properly. Let us meet at break; I will listen to all of it. For now we continue so we do not waste the class's time. You do not win with words, you win with time and calm. At break, let him speak fully first, then say your part. By then he has usually cooled and admits his share.
THAY: Phat, I hear you, and I want to hear it properly. Let us meet at break and I will listen to everything. For now I ask you to carry on the lesson with me.
TRO: (Silent for a moment.) ...Yes.
THAY: Thank you. At break I will be waiting for you.
VS: Arguing before an audience is how he defends his face. When you do not retort and do not demand an apology on the spot, he has nothing to push against. Arranging a private meeting gives him the right to be heard, and in private he is usually much gentler.
RG: You must really meet and really listen. A promised meeting not kept ruins everything. If he uses seriously insulting words, still meet him but say clearly that such language is unacceptable.
BT: Use gestures instead of words: raise a hand for silence and nod to arrange the meeting.
CN: I hear you. Let us meet at break so I can listen properly.

## A student glares, stays silent, will not answer
LOI: You call Thao's name; she sits silent, scowling, a defiant look. You ask again and still no answer. The whole class turns to look. You do not know what she is angry about.
CHIEU: Choose one of three | Do not force her to speak. Hand over a small slip with three lines to tick: one, I am angry about something at school; two, there is something going on at home; three, I want to be left alone today. She only needs to choose a number and push the slip toward you. You respect her answer and adjust. If she picks three, make a light note and check in a day later.
THAY: Thao, you do not need to answer aloud. Choose one of these three and push the slip to me, that is enough. I will respect it.
TRO: (Marks number one, discreetly.)
THAY: Thank you. If you want to say more at break, I will be at my desk.
VS: A silent student often does not know what to say or fears being criticised for saying it. Three options reduce the difficulty of opening her mouth and give her the feeling of having a choice. You get information without pressure.
RG: Do not use it as a way to interrogate. Respect the answer three. If two or three repeats over days, talk further and keep watch.
BT: She may add a line if she wants, or you replace the slip with three faces, a smile, a frown, a flat mouth, to point at.
CN: You do not need to speak. Choose one of these three and let me know.

## A student stubbornly denies: I did not do it
LOI: A chair is broken and three students sit around it. Each says it was not them. You ask a third time, and Hao stonewalls: I did not do it. Nobody owns up, but you feel Hao knows something.
CHIEU: A three-line agreement | Do not force confession. Say: I will stop hunting for anyone. The chair needs mending and I want all three of you to help. Draw up a three-line agreement: one, whoever did it, I will not name; two, the three of you will take the chair with me to the maintenance man; three, if the chair breaks again, all three tell the truth. They sign so that all share responsibility. When the pressure of being hunted lifts, the one responsible often owns up privately.
THAY: I will stop hunting for anyone. The chair needs fixing, so I ask the three of you to take it to the caretaker with me. Sign this, and whoever did it, I will not name.
TRO: (Hao, in a small voice.) Sir... it was me, I kicked it by accident.
THAY: Thank you, Hao, for saying it. I will keep it between us. Now let us fix it together.
VS: Pressing for the culprit scares the one responsible and makes him deny more. Lifting the pressure while keeping group responsibility gives him an honourable exit. When telling the truth is respected, he learns he need not be stubborn to protect himself.
RG: Do not use this when the damage is serious or someone is hurt. Do not promise secrecy and then make it public. If nobody owns up, do not punish all three, just mend it together.
BT: If he owns up privately, ask for a small act of repair, such as helping the caretaker wipe the chair.
CN: I will stop hunting for anyone. The three of you sign this agreement and help me fix the chair.

## A student says the teacher teaches badly
LOI: In the middle of the lesson, Viet says in a scathing tone: this is so hard to understand, how is anyone supposed to follow? A few laugh. You feel your heart sink.
CHIEU: A feedback box with conditions | Smile and say: thank you, I really need to hear that. Can you help me with a three-line feedback slip: one thing I do well, one specific part that is hard to follow, and one change you would like me to make. Write it privately and hand it in at the end of the week. Promise to reply. He now has a more concrete way of speaking, you have real information, and the class sees that you can listen.
THAY: Thank you for saying it straight. I need to hear the truth. Help me with a three-line feedback slip: what I do well, the specific part that is hard to follow, and one suggestion of yours.
TRO: Um... I will write it.
THAY: I promise to read it and reply. Where I can change, I will.
VS: A criticism can be valuable information, but the way it is said is rough. Asking for specifics turns jeering into feedback and teaches him how to give constructive responses. Your promise to reply shows you are not afraid of feedback.
RG: You must truly read and reply, even if it hurts. Do not use this to trap him. If he only wants to insult, state the limit.
BT: Open an anonymous feedback box for the whole class once a month and read aloud the kind suggestions.
CN: Thank you for saying it straight. Write me three specific lines of feedback and I will reply.

## A student suddenly walks out of the classroom
LOI: After being reminded, Duy jumps up, shoves his chair, walks out and slams the door. The whole class sits silent, looking at you. You are not sure how to react.
CHIEU: The door stays open | Do not chase and do not shout. Tell the class: we carry on, I will see him later. A little while on, quietly ask the class monitor or the caretaker to check that he is safe. At the end of the lesson go out and meet him, do not mention what happened, just say: I can see you are very angry; when you are ready we will talk. The classroom door is still open to him. Do not demand an apology at the first meeting.
THAY: Duy, I am here. I am not angry with you. When you are ready we will talk, today or tomorrow, whichever is fine.
TRO: (Silent a moment.) You scolded me in front of everyone.
THAY: I hear you. Thank you for telling me. I am sorry if I embarrassed you. Let us think together about how to avoid that next time.
VS: He leaves when his feelings are too big. Chasing him would inflame them further. Keeping the door open and meeting privately once he has cooled shows him you have not cut him off, and that you dare to own your part.
RG: You must ensure his safety when he leaves, and not let him leave the school. If he shows signs of self-harm, tell family and school at once. If you were at fault, own it truly, not just to get it over with.
BT: Prepare in advance a signal for him to step out calmly without saying a word, such as placing a green card on his desk.
CN: I am here whenever you are ready. The door is still open to you.
