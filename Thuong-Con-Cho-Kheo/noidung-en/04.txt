CHUONG: When your child brings home a bad mark
EPI: A poor test paper tells you where your child is stuck. It does not tell you who your child is.
LEAD: No evening is longer than the one when a child carries home a low mark. They have scolded themselves all the way, rehearsed excuses, and already guessed what their parents will say. What they have not guessed is that parents might respond differently. This chapter covers four kinds of bad marks, because each is its own illness and cannot be treated with one prescription.

## Your child hands you a three out of ten
BC: Long, in grade 6, hovers at the kitchen door for ten minutes before pulling a maths paper from his bag. Three. He hands it to Mum with his eyes on the floor tiles.
PX: Three! What kind of studying is this? We give you everything and you bring home a three. No phone from today! | A low mark means Mum stops loving me. Next time hide the paper, or change the mark, anything so she doesn't see.
ME: (Takes the paper, looks for a moment.) You gave this to me yourself. That must have taken a lot of courage.
CON: I was scared you'd shout.
ME: Honestly, I am worried. But I can see you are sadder than I am. How did you feel when the teacher handed it back?
CON: I wanted to cry. Only Tu and I were below average in the whole class.
ME: So you've been embarrassed since then. Well, the mark is what it is. Let's see what it says. Show me: which questions did you know how to do but got wrong, and which did you not know at all?
CON: Questions 1 and 2 I knew, but I mixed up the signs. From question 3 on I didn't understand what was being asked.
ME: Then there are two different things. Mixing up signs means practising care. Not understanding from question 3 means we have a gap. Do you remember which week that part was taught?
CON: The week I had a fever and missed three days.
ME: Ah. So there is a real reason, and it isn't that you're stupid. This weekend we'll catch up on that part; I'll ask your teacher for the material.
VS: A child bringing home a poor mark is already afraid and ashamed. In that state the brain only copes; it learns nothing more from scolding. Acknowledging the courage it took to hand over the paper protects the most precious habit: telling the truth. Then splitting the paper into knew it but slipped and did not know turns a frightening number into concrete things that can be done.
BH: Punishing a bad mark only teaches a child to hide marks. Going through the paper together teaches them to learn.
LN: Take a recent test and go through it with your child using two coloured pens: one colour for questions they knew but got wrong, another for those they did not know. Make a plan only for the second colour.
CN: You dared to show me this paper, and I value that. Now let's see what it is trying to tell us.
TN: In our home, is it safer to show a bad mark or to hide it?
TN: Do I know in which subject, and since when, my child has gaps?

## Your child tried their hardest and still scored low
BC: Last week Ngoc, in grade 9, stayed up until midnight three nights in a row revising literature. The paper comes back: five and a half. She throws down her bag and shuts her door. Mum hears crying.
PX: Stop crying, it's nothing to cry about. Just try harder next time. | Mum does not understand. I already tried as hard as I can; how could I try more? I must really be stupid.
ME: (Knocks, brings in a glass of water.) May I sit here? I won't say anything.
CON: (Crying.) I studied three nights, Mum. Three nights! I knew all of it.
ME: I saw you stay up. Putting in that much and getting this result must feel so unfair.
CON: I really am stupid. The others play around and still get seven or eight.
ME: I don't think so. A stupid person is one who won't sit down at the desk; you sat there three nights. My guess is you put in enough effort, but in the wrong place. When the sting eases, we'll look at the teacher's comments. No hurry.
CON: (After a while.) The teacher wrote that I copied model essays and had no ideas of my own. But I memorised model essays to be safe...
ME: So you are very good at memorising, but the teacher is marking something else. The question asked for one thing and we trained for another. That can be fixed, and it does not need later nights.
VS: A child who gives everything and still fails stands at a dangerous fork: concluding that effort is useless. Try harder accidentally confirms that fear, because it implies the effort was not enough. What the child needs to hear is that the effort was real and was seen, and that what must change is the method. Separating effort from method preserves the belief that the result is still within their control.
BH: To a child who has already given everything, do not say try harder. Say: your effort is enough; let's find another way.
LN: Ask your child how they revise one particular subject: what they do first, what next, how they know they have learned it. Only ask and listen. Often, saying it aloud lets them see where it went off course.
CN: I can see how much work you put in. Your effort is enough; now we change the method.
TN: Do I know how my child studies, or only how long they sit?
TN: When I give everything and fail, what words from others comfort me most?

## Your child hides marks or changes them
BC: Dad opens the school app and finds three below-average marks this month for his grade 7 son. Asked last week, Quan said there had been no tests. In his bag is a paper on which a 4 has been altered to a 9.
PX: You dare to deceive your own father? A bad mark I could forgive, but dishonesty I will not. So young and already a crook! | I am a crook. And Dad saying he forgives bad marks is a lie, because last time he did not.
BA: (Waits until after dinner, calls him out to the balcony.) Quan, I've looked at the school app. I've also seen the paper in your bag. I haven't asked anything yet; you speak first.
CON: (A very long silence.) ...I'm sorry, Dad.
BA: I accept the apology. I am not happy that you changed the mark; I'll say that plainly. But first I want to understand one thing: when you picked up the pen to change that 4, what were you most afraid of?
CON: That you'd be disappointed. Last time I got a 5 you didn't speak to me for two days.
BA: ...In those two days I thought I was teaching you. Hearing you now, I see I was teaching you to be afraid of me. That part is my fault.
CON: I didn't want to trick you. I just didn't know what to do.
BA: Then we both start again. My part: from now on I will sit and listen to any mark, without that kind of silence. Your part: you take this paper to your teacher and tell the truth yourself. And real marks, from now on, you tell me truthfully. Agreed?
CON: Yes. Will you come with me to see the teacher?
BA: I'll stand outside the door. You do the talking.
VS: Children almost never lie about marks because they are dishonest by nature. They lie when the price of the truth at home is higher than the price of a lie. Silence, coldness and explosions are all very high prices for a child. If you want the truth, make it cheaper: telling the truth brings help, while the consequences of the dishonesty are for the child to repair, with a parent standing close.
BH: Children lie when the truth costs too much. Your task is to lower the price of the truth, not to raise the penalty for lying.
LN: Make one clear promise and keep it at all costs: Whatever mark you tell me honestly, I will not shout; we will just go over it together. The first time your child tests that promise with a bad mark is the moment that decides everything.
CN: When you changed that mark, what were you most afraid of?
TN: The last time my child told me an unpleasant truth, how did I react?
TN: Have I ever punished my child with silence, refusing to look at them?

## Your child fails an important exam
BC: At ten in the morning the grade 10 entrance results appear. Thu is half a mark short of her first-choice school. The whole family had been sure she would pass. She sits in front of the screen, not crying, not speaking. Mum's phone begins to ring: relatives asking.
PX: I told you to revise maths properly and you didn't listen. What am I supposed to tell your grandparents now? | I have shamed the family. Mum is hurting for herself, not for me.
ME: (Silences the phone, turns it face down. Sits beside her, arm around her shoulders.) I'm here.
CON: (Very quietly.) I'm sorry, Mum and Dad.
ME: You have nothing to apologise for. You studied all year; I saw every night of it. Half a mark short hurts so much, I know.
CON: All my friends passed. I can't even open the class chat.
ME: Then don't open it today. I'll handle whoever calls; you don't have to answer anyone. Today you only need to be sad, as sad as you need.
CON: So where will I go to school now?
ME: We still have the second choice and several other routes; Dad and I have looked, and none of them is a dead end. But that is for the day after tomorrow. Right now I'll make you a bowl of noodles; eat whatever you can.
CON: Are you disappointed in me?
ME: I am sad because you are sad. Disappointed in you, no. Half a mark cannot change the fact that you are my child.
VS: After a big failure a child carries three layers of pain: regret for themselves, shame before friends, and fear of having let their parents down. The third is often the heaviest and the only one parents can lift at once. The first two days are not for analysing causes or discussing options. They are for shielding the child from well-meaning questions, feeding them, letting them sleep and letting them grieve. Plans only go in once the feelings have somewhere to go.
BH: On the day your child fails, they do not need your analysis. They need to know their place in your heart is intact.
LN: If you have a child with an exam coming, tell them tonight: Pass or fail, dinner at our house stays the same. We have other routes ready; just sit the exam with a free mind. Said before the exam, it is worth ten times more than after.
CN: I am sad because you are sad. Disappointed in you, no.
TN: If my child fails, what do I fear most: their future, or what people will say?
TN: Have we prepared a second and third option together, or is the whole family staked on one door?
