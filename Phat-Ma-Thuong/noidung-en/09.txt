CHUONG: Copying, cribbing, cheating
EPI: A student who copies is seldom bad. He copies out of fear. Remove the fear first, then deal with the behaviour.
LEAD: Cheating is the gravest breach of school ethics, and also the fault teachers handle wrongly most easily, because heavy punishment makes students hide it better, while ignoring it tells others that cheating pays. This chapter offers ways to hold the principle very clearly yet humanely enough that the student can mend, and honest students are not disadvantaged.

## A student copies a friend's homework before class
LOI: Every morning you see several students crowd round a strong student's notebook to copy before class. One copies word for word, even the spelling errors. The friend lets them copy and laughs, saying he is helping.
CHIEU: Copy and understand | Do not make him copy again. Call the student who copied aside: choose one answer you copied and explain one step to me in your own words. If he can, he learned something from copying and you acknowledge it. If not, you sit with him for five minutes and explain exactly that line again. From then he sees that copying is a longer, more tiring route than learning.
THAY: Pick one answer you copied this morning and explain one step to me in your own words. If you cannot, that is fine; I will explain it again right away.
TRO: Um... I do not understand this step, sir.
THAY: Thank you for being honest. Let us sit for five minutes and redo it.
VS: Copying often happens when a student lacks time or finds the work hard. A chance to move from copying to understanding one line lets him see the gap in his knowledge. This move does not punish, it removes the point of copying.
RG: Do not publicly name the student who copied. Do not use it when he copies from heavy overload, since then the workload needs support. Do not blame the friend who let him copy before the class.
BT: Set up a corner for early work: ten minutes at the start of the morning where students do homework together with you sitting among them.
CN: Explain one step you copied for me, in your own words. If you do not understand it yet, I will explain it again.

## A student cheats during a test
LOI: You see Bao glance at his neighbour's paper twice. By the third, you are certain. He knows he has been caught, his face white and his hand trembling. Nobody else in the class has noticed.
CHIEU: Two rounds | Quietly turn his paper face down and whisper: I am keeping this paper; carry on a fresh sheet. At the end, keep him back privately: this time you did the work, I keep the result at the level of the genuine paper. Three days later he redoes an equivalent part in ten minutes in front of you so you know how much he really understands. The second mark is the true one. Ask: what made you want to look at your friend's paper? If he says the truth, you find a way together.
THAY: Bao, I am keeping this paper; carry on with a new sheet. Stay a moment at the end, I want to ask you something, not to scold you.
TRO: Um... I am sorry, sir. I was afraid of a low mark, and my parents would hit me.
THAY: I understand. Thank you for telling me the truth. I will not let this spread. Let us find a way to revise so you feel more confident.
VS: Cheating is often a sign of fear, and the fear will recur if only the behaviour is punished. Offering a chance to redo it with you present keeps the principle and gives him a path. He learns that telling the truth does not destroy him.
RG: If school rules set a penalty, follow them but keep a humane tone. Do not mock, do not tell the class. If the fear comes from violence at home, the teacher needs to give discreet support, and should not tell the family at once.
BT: Have him write a short note: if I could redo it, what would I prepare and whom would I ask for help.
CN: I am keeping this paper. Stay a moment, I want to ask you something, not to scold.

## A strong student lets a friend copy and calls it helping
LOI: Hien let her close friend copy a test because she could not bring herself to refuse. The friend was later caught and named Hien. Hien cries and says she only wanted to help.
CHIEU: Give the fishing rod | Tell Hien privately: your friendship is precious, but letting her copy is giving a fish, not a fishing rod. Your task: for two sessions you sit and teach her friend exactly that part, with me beside you observing. After you finish, measure the result with a short paper of hers, not marked. Hien sees what real help means, and the other student actually learns.
THAY: Hien, I understand you love your friend. But letting her copy is giving a fish. For the next two sessions you will teach her that exact part, and I will sit beside you.
TRO: Um... I can teach her, sir. But will she be angry with me?
THAY: I believe she will understand. Let us tell her together that you help in a different way.
VS: Students who let others copy usually act out of friendship, not dishonesty. This keeps the friendship and changes the form of help, so she grasps the line between real and wrong help. The other student also truly learns.
RG: Do not publicise the copying. Do not let Hien bear the responsibility alone for her friend. The mark for the cheated paper is still handled by regulation.
BT: Form pairs of buddies who help each other learn in permitted ways.
CN: Letting a friend copy is giving a fish. You teach her that lesson and I will sit beside you.

## A student copies an online text word for word
LOI: Phong's major assignment has a very good paragraph but a voice strangely unlike his. You suspect he copied it from the internet or used a tool to write it. He says he wrote it himself.
CHIEU: Read aloud, then ask | Do not accuse. Invite him to read the paragraph aloud to you privately. Ask three questions: what is the main idea, why did you choose this word, and if you rewrote it in your own words how would you say it? Where he can explain, the passage stays. Where he cannot, he rewrites in his own words within three days. You never ask whether he copied, only whether he understands.
THAY: Phong, please read this paragraph aloud to me. Then I will ask three questions: what is the main idea, why did you choose this word, and how would you say it in your own words?
TRO: Um... I referred to other sources for this paragraph, sir, I did not understand all of it.
THAY: Thank you for being honest. Rewrite it in your own words within three days and I will read it.
VS: Not accusing, but letting him face his own understanding. He sees the gap without feeling caught red-handed. Shifting the focus from whether he copied to whether he understands teaches the spirit of real learning.
RG: If school rules forbid it strictly and he copied verbatim, you must still follow procedure. Do not use the questions as a trap. Respect his explanation if reasonable.
BT: From now on add a two-minute oral presentation step to major assignments.
CN: Read this paragraph aloud to me, then tell me in your own words.
