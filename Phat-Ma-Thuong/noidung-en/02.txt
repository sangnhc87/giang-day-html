CHUONG: A noisy class: when the fault is collective
EPI: A noisy class is not the enemy. It is energy that has not yet found somewhere to go.
LEAD: Punishing one student is easy. Punishing the whole class is hard, because collective punishment so easily becomes unfair to the well-behaved. This chapter gathers moves that bring the whole class back to its rhythm without shouting or slamming the desk, and without making any one student carry the blame for others. What they share: the class adjusts itself, and you only keep the beat.

## The class erupts after break
LOI: The bell for class has rung, yet the room is like a market. You walk in and many are still standing and talking, one is running up from the back. Each time it takes five or seven minutes to settle them, out of a forty-five-minute lesson.
CHIEU: The seconds account | Walk in without a word and quietly write a T on the corner of the board, then start recording the seconds the class takes to settle. When all are seated and quiet, fix the number, say 74 seconds. Say: the class owes me 74 seconds. At the end of the lesson do not keep them in; instead they repay by standing quietly and reading aloud a line of poetry or a formula from today. The next day the number usually drops sharply, because the class has seen its own figure.
THAY: I am not shouting. I am only recording. The class owes me seventy-four seconds. At the end of the lesson we repay it by reading today's formula aloud.
TRO: Yes, we will repay it.
THAY: Tomorrow I will record again. I expect the number to be smaller.
VS: A class gets loud because nobody can measure it. A figure on the board makes everyone feel measured, while no one is scolded. Repaying the debt by recalling knowledge turns the punishment into a review.
RG: Do not write individual names on the board. Do not stretch the repayment beyond three minutes. If one student repeatedly ignores reminders, deal with that student privately instead of making the class carry it.
BT: Change it to an hourglass on your desk: if the sand runs out before the class is quiet, at the end of the lesson the class gives you a round of applause for your patience.
CN: I am not angry, I am only counting. Let us repay it ourselves.

## Late afternoon: tired and restless at once
LOI: Last lesson of the afternoon, hot weather, the class both worn out and fidgety. Speak loudly and it gets noisier, speak softly and nobody hears. The bell and the thumping of the desk no longer work.
CHIEU: The teacher whispers | Stop teaching, walk to the middle of the room and speak very softly, almost whispering. Those who can hear will concentrate; those who cannot will have to be quiet to hear. Teach like this for five minutes, then ask a small question, also whispered. If the class stays noisy, stop completely, smile and wait. The class quiets down out of curiosity about what you are about to say.
THAY: (Very softly, smiling.) From now on I will speak quietly. Whoever wants to hear, please be quiet for me. This is the most important part of the lesson.
TRO: (Whispering back.) Yes.
THAY: Good. First question, quietly: how many axes of symmetry does a circle have?
VS: Loud sound makes the whole class raise its voice. Lowering yours makes them lower theirs. This move punishes no one, but it removes the point of the noise because there is no contest of volume.
RG: Do not use it when the noise comes from a scuffle or real conflict. Then stop completely and deal with that first.
BT: Put a small sign reading SHHH on the board; when the class is quiet you turn it round to show Thank you.
CN: I speak softly for those who truly want to hear.

## The whole class drums on the desks to tease you
LOI: As you walk in, the whole class drums on the desks in rhythm. It looks like a joke planned in advance. It seems fun, but you sense it is a small test of authority.
CHIEU: The conductor | Stand still and smile, then raise your hand like a professional conductor and conduct that very drumming: faster, slower, louder, softer, then stop abruptly with a closed fist. After a minute bow: thank you, orchestra, the performance is over. The class understands that you know the joke, play along, but hold the baton.
THAY: Oh, does our class have an orchestra? Let me conduct it properly. Faster... slower... and stop!
TRO: (Laughing.) The teacher is joining in!
THAY: Thank you, orchestra. Now on to the next piece: Functions.
VS: A test of nerve usually waits for an angry reaction. When you absorb it and take the baton, the game loses its drama because there is nothing left to push against. Students remember you as someone who can joke and still keep the beat.
RG: Use only when the drumming is a shared joke, not meant to insult. If the beat comes with mockery of you or a classmate, stop and speak plainly and calmly.
BT: Invite a skilled student to be conductor next time while you sit as audience.
CN: I love this orchestra. Let me conduct a little and then we start the lesson.

## Study period: the teacher steps out and the class gets loud
LOI: You leave the room for five minutes to take a call or fetch papers, and come back to a class as noisy as a market. The class monitor wrote down the names of some who talked, but those students now resent him.
CHIEU: Quiet green dots | Put up a big chart with ten small squares. Each time you are out and the class stays quiet, the class itself colours one green dot. With ten green dots, the class gets a small reward you choose from the class's own proposals, such as ten minutes of music or a story from you. If the class is noisy, there is no dot but no deduction either; just wait for next time. The monitor writes no names, only reports yes or no.
THAY: From now on, each time I step out and the class keeps quiet by itself, you earn a green dot. Ten green dots and the class chooses a reward. I do not need anyone to write down names. I only need the class to hold itself.
TRO: Yes. Any reward at all?
THAY: The class discusses, I approve, as long as it is fun and wholesome.
VS: It gives responsibility to the group without pushing one student into the role of police. The reward is small but progress is visible, so the class feels positive peer pressure. Not deducting dots keeps the class from losing heart.
RG: Do not turn the monitor into an informer. Do not use costly rewards or ones that eat into lesson time.
BT: Replace dots with puzzle pieces; ten pieces and a picture of the whole class appears.
CN: I trust our class to hold itself. Ten green dots and the class chooses the reward.

## A strange noise and nobody owns up
LOI: In the middle of a silent lesson, a sound like a cat meowing comes from the back. The whole class laughs. You look round but nobody admits it. Every student looks the other way.
CHIEU: Invited to perform at break | Do not hunt for the culprit. Smile and say: that sounded so real I am impressed. Whoever did it, come to me at break and perform it once more for me, then next time save the talent for the school show. No naming, no investigation. Usually one student, or two, will come, having already been stared at by the class.
THAY: That sounded so real it startled me. Whoever did it, come at break and do it once more for me, I want to hear it properly. For now, back to the lesson.
TRO: (At break, embarrassed.) Um... sir, it was me.
THAY: Thank you for coming. It was a very good sound. Next time, save it for the show.
VS: Students own up more easily when they do not have to do it before the class and when you do not seem offended. This takes away the challenge in the act, and leads the student to a private meeting, where the gentlest talk happens.
RG: Do not force anyone to confess. If nobody comes, laugh it off. Do not punish the whole class to find the culprit.
BT: Open a small talent slot at the end of the week: anyone with an odd gift, like mimicry or magic, performs for two minutes.
CN: That was quite a talent. Whoever did it, come at break and perform it for me privately.
